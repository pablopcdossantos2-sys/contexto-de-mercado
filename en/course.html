Market Context Course

A compact English version of the ten core modules.

1. What market context is

Context is the frame that gives meaning to price behavior. Separate context, scenario, trigger and invalidation. The same signal can mean different things in different regimes and locations.

2. Trend, range and transition

A regime is a description, not a permanent label. Trend emphasizes directional efficiency, range emphasizes repeated return to an area, and transition describes loss of the prior regime's characteristics.

3. Top-down reading

Start with the decision horizon, read the larger structure, mark only meaningful references, then move to the execution chart. The goal is hierarchy, not more timeframes.

4. Session opening and timing

The opening, middle of the session, scheduled events and closing can have different liquidity and volatility. Speed is not the same as clarity.

5. Price location and references

Previous highs/lows, prior close, range edges and VWAP can be contextual references. A reference matters only if it changes what you observe or how you plan.

6. Volatility

Volatility describes magnitude and pace, not direction. Compression and expansion change the meaning of fixed distances, stops and reaction time.

7. Liquidity, volume and aggression

Volume is executed activity; aggression describes initiative in execution; passive liquidity is what is offered before execution. None of them gives direct access to participant intent.

8. Gaps, acceptance and rejection

A breakout is not enough. Observe whether price can remain in the new area. Failed acceptance can be more informative than the visual break itself.

9. Correlation and intermarket

Correlation is association, not causation. Intermarket relationships can help when they are economically meaningful and stable enough to be useful.

10. Macroeconomic events

The calendar is primarily a risk-and-timing tool. You do not need to predict the number or the market reaction; define behavior before the event.

Remember: context reduces blind decisions; it does not eliminate uncertainty or risk.
